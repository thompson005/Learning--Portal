Active Directory

Active Directory is a directory service created by Microsoft for managing computers and other devices on a network. It helps organizations keep track of user accounts, computers, and various online resources.

Active Directory also facilitates centralized management of security policies and permissions, ensuring that users have appropriate access to resources while maintaing the integrity of the network.

Example Use Cases

In an office environment a company has hundreds of employees using computers and shared resources like printers and file servers.

With Active Directory, administrators can:

Create accounts for employees and assign them roles.

Automatically grant or restrict access to shared folders, printers, or specific applications based on their job roles.

Reset passwords or disable accounts when employees leave, all from a central control panel.

## Slide 5

Think of Active Directory like the reception desk at a large office building:

Receptionist (Active Directory): Maintains a list of people (users) who work there, their
job titles (roles), and where they are allowed to go (permissions).

Access Badges (Authentication): Employees swipe their badges at the door (login), and
the receptionist checks if they are allowed inside (are they on the list?).

Restricted Areas (Authorization): Certain employees can enter only specific floors or
rooms (resources), depending on their job roles.
